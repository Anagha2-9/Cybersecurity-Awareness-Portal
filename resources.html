Interactive Accordions

1. Do NOT click any links, open attachments, or reply to the email.
2. Forward the suspicious email to your institution's IT helpdesk or security operations center.
3. If you accidentally clicked a link and entered credentials, immediately change your password from another known clean device and notify your administrators.

Two-Factor Authentication requires two distinct authentication factors: something you know (your password) plus something you have (an authenticator app on your smartphone or a physical security key). Even if attackers purchase your leaked password on the dark web, they cannot access your account without physical access to your second factor.

Open public Wi-Fi networks do not require credentials to join, which means malicious actors on the same network can intercept unencrypted network packets or deploy "Evil Twin" fake hotspots to intercept sensitive browsing data. Always ensure websites use HTTPS or tunnel your traffic through a trusted VPN.

Symmetric encryption (such as AES) uses the same secret key to both encrypt and decrypt data—fast and ideal for bulk file storage. Asymmetric encryption (such as RSA or ECC) uses a mathematically linked key pair: a public key for encryption and a private key for decryption, which forms the backbone of secure internet traffic via HTTPS/TLS.

Essential Cybersecurity Glossary for Beginners

Key terminology every student and digital citizen should recognize:

Term Category Plain-English Definition
Zero-Day Vulnerability Threat A software flaw unknown to the vendor, for which zero days of patching exist.
Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Defense Requiring two or more verification proofs before granting access to an account.
Credential Stuffing Attack Automated testing of stolen username/password pairs across hundreds of websites.
Brute-Force Attack Attack Submitting millions of password combinations until the correct one is discovered.
Virtual Private Network (VPN) Privacy An encrypted tunnel connecting your device to a remote server across public networks.
