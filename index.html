Phishing Attacks

High Risk

Deceptive emails, fake login portals, or fraudulent text messages designed to trick you into revealing passwords or payment details.

Warning Sign: Urgent deadlines, unfamiliar sender domains, and fake account suspension warnings.

🔒

Ransomware

Critical

Malware that encrypts your files and locks your computer, demanding an extortion payment to restore access to your irreplaceable data.

Primary Defense: Maintain automated offline backups using the 3-2-1 backup strategy.

🔑

Credential Attacks

Frequent

Automated brute-force guessing, credential stuffing from public data breaches, and dictionary attacks against weak or reused passwords.

Primary Defense: Use 14+ character passphrases, a password manager, and two-factor authentication (2FA).

🧠

Social Engineering

Psychological

Manipulating human psychology rather than exploiting software vulnerabilities, tricking victims into handing over OTPs and private credentials.

Warning Sign: Callers claiming to be bank or college staff demanding immediate password resets.

Essential Cyber Hygiene Checklist

Implement these foundational rules across your personal and academic accounts:

  • Activate Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on your email, student portal, and banking accounts.
  • Never reuse passwords across multiple websites; a breach on one site compromises all of them.
  • Inspect website URLs carefully for HTTPS (padlock icon) and check for typosquatted domain names.
  • Keep your operating system and browsers updated with automatic security patches enabled.
  • Avoid open public Wi-Fi for sensitive banking transactions unless connected via a trusted VPN.

Incident Response Protocol

What to do immediately if you suspect an account compromise:

  1. Disconnect the affected device from Wi-Fi and ethernet networks to stop malware spread.
  2. Change account passwords immediately using another known clean device.
  3. Revoke active sessions and tokens in your account security settings.
  4. Notify your institution's IT helpdesk or bank support to monitor unauthorized activity.
  5. Scan your system using updated endpoint antivirus tools and review recent login logs.

The Core Pillars of Information Security (CIA Triad)

All international cybersecurity frameworks (NIST, ISO 27001) revolve around these three fundamental principles:

1. Confidentiality

Ensuring that sensitive information is accessible only to authorized people and processes through access controls, encryption, and strict authentication.

2. Integrity

Guaranteeing that data remains authentic, accurate, and completely safeguarded against unauthorized modification, deletion, or tampering in transit and storage.

3. Availability

Ensuring systems, networks, applications, and databases remain accessible to authorized users reliably whenever required.
