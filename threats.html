Common Cyber Threats & Attack Vectors

Cybercriminals exploit both technical flaws and human cognitive biases. Explore the primary threat vectors targeting internet users, understand their key warning signs, and learn how to proactively protect your systems.

🎣

Phishing Attacks

High Severity

What it is: Fraudulent communications (emails, SMS, direct messages, or fake websites) crafted to mimic trusted entities like banks, universities, or tech companies to steal credentials and sensitive data.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • Mismatched sender email domains (e.g. support@paypa1-security.com).
  • Manufactured urgency: "Immediate action required or account suspended!"
  • Generic greetings such as "Dear Customer" instead of your actual name.
  • Hyperlinks pointing to unfamiliar, unverified domain URLs upon hover.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Inspect sender email headers and hover over links before clicking.
  • Never disclose passwords, OTPs, or credit card CVV codes over email.
  • Enforce hardware or app-based Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
🦠

Malware & Spyware

High Severity

What it is: Any malicious software—including viruses, trojans, keyloggers, and spyware—engineered to infiltrate, disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems and personal files.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • Sudden system slowdowns, high CPU/RAM usage, and frequent crashes.
  • Persistent browser redirects and intrusive pop-up advertisements.
  • Antivirus tools and Windows security settings disabled unexpectedly.
  • Unfamiliar software icons appearing on your desktop or system tray.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Download binaries and installers only from verified official vendor websites.
  • Keep operating system security patches and browser updates current.
  • Avoid pirated software, cracked games, and unverified torrent files.
🔒

Ransomware Extortion

Critical

What it is: A severe form of malware that secretly encrypts a victim's documents, databases, and photos with unbreakable cryptographic algorithms, demanding ransom payments (typically in cryptocurrency) for decryption.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • File extensions abruptly changing (e.g., report.pdf.locked).
  • Inability to open personal files, accompanied by desktop wallpaper changes.
  • Ransom demand instructions and countdown timers saved on your desktop.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Follow the 3-2-1 backup strategy: 3 copies, on 2 media types, with 1 offline.
  • Block executable attachments (.exe, .vbs, .bat) in email filters.
  • Use endpoint protection with real-time ransomware behavior detection.
🔑

Password & Credential Attacks

Frequent

What it is: Systematic attempts to crack passwords using automated brute-force dictionaries, credential stuffing (using leaked credentials from past breaches), and keylogging malware.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • Automated "New login from unknown device/location" alert notifications.
  • Frequent lockouts caused by failed login attempts you did not trigger.
  • Password reset emails received unexpectedly for accounts you own.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Construct 14+ character passphrases composed of random words.
  • Never reuse passwords across personal, college, or financial accounts.
  • Store complex credentials in a reputable password manager.
🧠

Social Engineering

Psychological

What it is: Psychological manipulation designed to trick individuals into divulging confidential information, granting access to restricted portals, or performing financial transfers under false pretenses.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • High-pressure tactics insisting on bypassing standard safety verification.
  • Unsolicited calls from "IT Support" or "Bank Representatives" asking for OTPs.
  • Tailgating: strangers requesting access through secure office doorways.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Always verify suspicious requests via trusted out-of-band contact channels.
  • Remember: legitimate banks and IT administrators will never request your OTP or password.
  • Slow down: pause and question unexpected urgent requests.
🌊

Denial of Service (DDoS)

Availability

What it is: A malicious attempt to disrupt the normal traffic of a targeted server, service, or network by overwhelming the target with an artificial flood of internet traffic from compromised botnets.

⚠️ Common Warning Signs:

  • Severe network and server latency or complete unresponsiveness.
  • Sudden unexplained traffic spikes originating from geographically dispersed IP pools.
  • Repetitive 502/504 gateway timeout errors on web applications.

🛡️ Prevention Methods:

  • Route application traffic through cloud Web Application Firewalls (WAF).
  • Implement rate limiting, Anycast DNS routing, and DDoS mitigation proxies.
  • Maintain redundant network pathways and scalable server infrastructure.
